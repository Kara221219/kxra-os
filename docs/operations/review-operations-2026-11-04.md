# Independent eligibility review operations — local production-wiring handoff

This isolated change addresses a concrete launch blocker: customer checkout and AI setup consume independent review records, but there was no operator application workflow to record or withdraw them. `/os/review-operations` and `/api/review-operations` now use verified internal KXRA owner identity, fresh MFA and an exact server-pinned policy to review only explicitly delegated customers, providers and review types. A reviewer cannot review a customer organisation they belong to or their own application. This is factual human review, not automated verification: a submitted evidence digest identifies an independently reviewed record and proves nothing by itself.

The console loads in its own asynchronous client chunk after the authorised page opens, keeping it out of the shared initial workspace bundle. The feature is off by default. No grants, policies, terms, financial settings, keys or provider permissions are created by the payload or application. Both `KXRA_REVIEW_OPERATIONS_ENABLED=true` and the exact approved `KXRA_REVIEW_OPERATIONS_POLICY_SHA256` are needed, as well as current scoped private grants. Neither flag supplies a grant. Customers, staff, workers and malformed authority inputs remain denied. The UI never requests raw documents, credentials, provider tokens or customer messages; strict response projections omit private intent fingerprints, account/evidence hashes, billing identifiers and frozen private context.

A grant binds the reviewer account and session, internal organisation, target customer, BUSINESS or AI purpose, provider, existing current policy, validity and maximum decision duration. Withdrawal and replacement are separate explicit capabilities, both default false. There is no application endpoint to create grants, enable policies or change their scope. Review decisions use server-resolved declaration/intent, billing binding, terms and policy IDs. Required attestation and evidence fields have no pre-cleared defaults. Business clearance does not mutate paid-access authority. AI clearance does not call a provider or establish provider acceptance. Current customer status/save/run gates continue to decide authority independently.

Each open request freezes the private context hash and a minimal public snapshot for at most fifteen minutes and within grant/policy validity. One immutable operation receipt completes a request transactionally with its exact decision. An identical operation can retrieve its historical receipt; altered payload or competing decisions conflict. A lost response disables further submission and retains request/operation references for read-only recovery. A historical receipt explicitly carries no current entitlement or provider acceptance. Expired, replaced or changed contexts require a new request; replacement of an existing decision requires its separate grant. Saved AI connections require a new customer intent for renewal.

Grant, request and subject locks precede fresh checks. Decisions fence reviewer and applicant account rows, existing memberships, platform/customer state and the current policy pointer. Reviewer session/member/platform revocation or newer policy activation while an operation waits on its subject is checked after the wait. Policy writes take the exclusive advisory transaction fence `hashtextextended('kxra-review-current-policy-v1',0)`; decisions take its shared side before ordered accounts, memberships, organisations and policy row locks. Privileged multi-object maintenance must follow that order or finish before invoking the operation. A deadlock/timeout aborts the transaction; no automatic decision retry occurs. Grant withdrawal prevents future operations and does not silently rewrite a factual review already committed. Review withdrawal or expiry stops subsequent customer authority checks; it cannot recall an already accepted external effect.

`database/production/review-operations-v1.sql` remains an **unnumbered review-only payload** after the reviewed genuine AI118 payload on exact production117. It is not a hosted migration or release authorization. Canonical fixture118/119 and the existing release guard are unchanged. The local acceptance runner removes only the hash-validated fixture exclusions in a new clone, builds a newly owned Unix-socket-only disposable117 cluster, installs the explicitly reviewed AI118 and operations payload, and uses synthetic data. It accepts no target URL or credential. Never apply it to an existing or hosted database based on local evidence.

## Required before activation

The owner must identify the independent human reviewers and approved factual evidence process, including permitted jurisdiction/provider decisions, record custody and retention/access responsibilities. An explicit grant must specify customer/type/provider/current policy, reviewer session, validity/decision duration, and whether withdrawal or replacement is permitted. No invented thirty-day review or retention rule is enacted. Existing prices, tax direction, customer terms and financial commitments remain unchanged; the previously unresolved payment-failure grace applicability remains a separate owner decision.

Engineering must turn this reviewed payload into a migration and manifest matching the actual target history, prove its upgrade/failure/recovery path under production roles, review grant provisioning/withdrawal runbooks and arrange approved secure evidence custody. Only then may a separately authorized release set the policy pin/flag and issue exact grants. This task performs none of those external actions.

Full requested launch scope remains required. Real AI credential custody and provider acceptance, production paired-agent tool isolation/authentication, real connector installation/vault/transport, custom-app production schema/recovery, hosted signup/email and payment delivery acceptance remain separate engineering/release gates. This workflow removes the missing operator app wiring locally; it does not make the full platform live or ready for revenue.
