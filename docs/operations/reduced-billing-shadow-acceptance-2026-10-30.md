This implements the event-led candidate beyond the earlier reference model. ## Implemented transport and durable storage

`packages/integrations/reduced-billing-shadow.ts` runs the existing preflight and money-field validators against a bounded per-attempt observation cache. Successful LIVE and sandbox tests measure **seven physical GETs**, not an assumed four/eight: account, subscription, invoice, price/product, invoice-payment allocations, PaymentIntent and charge. Repeated validator reads use the same copied bytes. Nothing is cached across attempts, accounts, revisions or processes. The existing catalogue, allocation, amount, GBP currency, mode, customer, subscription, period, consent provenance, refund/dispute and collected-payment checks remain. Real schema/version and hosted provider acceptance remain required; the API stays pinned to `2025-07-30.basil`.

The cache deliberately removes repeated network observations, so it does **not** preserve the old interleaving-detection behavior or provide an atomic provider snapshot. A passing adversarial test explicitly demonstrates a provider-side refund after the charge's one observation being invisible without an event/current-state reread. Shadow observations are labelled `REDUCED_SHADOW_V1` and `entitlement_authority:false`; they cannot be submitted as an ordinary paid-proof packet. This is why production adoption still needs an explicit financial-freshness/security decision.

Historical consent comes only from an immutable original strict-worker ELIGIBLE receipt. The worker verifies the original canonical packet SHA, scope, org/customer/subscription, checkout/session, price/product, mapping, exact policy ID/revision and terms/presentation hashes. SQL pins its source job/hash/consent hash into private immutable storage, with current member/legal/policy checks. A changed checkout, policy or terms cannot reuse it. No client declaration or positive webhook can manufacture assent or bootstrap an unknown/never-paid purchase.

`supabase/proposals/reduced-billing-shadow.sql` adds default-empty, RLS-protected config, consent pins, one coalesced job per scope/workspace and append-only observations. No new roles, keys or customer grants are created. An insertion trigger attaches to the **existing verified durable webhook inbox transaction**. Receipt/digest and revision changes commit before acknowledgement; failure rolls back and produces 503. Duplicate events retain their original effect. Existing binding generation changes invalidate old paid authority; timestamps do not order events. Customer/subscription conflicts are held for review. Events with no reliable canonical customer/subscription association remain HELD in the existing inbox; this candidate does not automatically resolve every refund/dispute payload variant. Those cases receive no immediate-mapping guarantee and must be treated like delayed evidence until verified resolution or the hard financial-age deadline. The shadow queue cannot consume the authoritative queue's claim/ACK.

Separate genuine-runner RPCs use fixed execution scope, per-binding locks, bounded SKIP LOCKED discovery, expiring claim tokens and generation CAS. They select only consumed checkouts with prior strict paid/consent receipts. A successful shadow observation leaves every production paid-proof field unchanged. Failure uses durable exponential backoff plus bounded Retry-After; five failed/expired attempts enter DEAD for operator handling. A new genuinely different event may schedule a new generation; it cannot grant access.

Every attempt atomically reserves **seven** reads against the existing shared monthly and conservative 31-day counter, under the same account-scope lock as the old scheduler. Unused permits remain charged after errors/crash. No budget is seeded or enabled. This avoids losing 25 unused credits from the older 32-permit batch on every short invocation. A run claims at most three workspaces and starts at most four provider reads/second locally, with no catch-up burst after a stall. This is not a distributed per-second limiter for unrelated clients: keep one operator-owned invocation topology and monitor whole-account traffic before increasing concurrency. All provider destinations, headers, deadlines, bytes/chunks, redirect policy and finite error output remain bounded.

## Plain-language recommendation and decision

Prefer durable event-led reconciliation with a hard age limit over buying an exceptional polling allowance. Do not activate the shadow cache as financial authority merely because its tests pass. For a concrete owner/security review, consider **at most 24 hours for ordinary work by an already verified paid subscriber**, with immediate known-event/local suspension and stricter on-demand verification for initial purchase, additional seats and other specifically designated consequential actions. That is the only listed interval whose periodic 16-workspace baseline comfortably fits the ordinary allocation floor. It deliberately accepts the possibility of **up to one day of continued ordinary paid-feature use after an entirely missed refund/cancellation/dispute**, capped earlier by paid-through. If that exposure is unacceptable, choose a shorter bound and a measured smaller workload or verified sufficient allocation; do not silently relax the existing policy or declare a larger customer cap approved.

Local admin/member/legal suspension is independent of Stripe and must deny at the next authoritative SQL action check. A received, successfully mapped relevant webhook invalidates the canonical revision in its inbox transaction, without waiting for the periodic sweep. Provider-side changes whose events are lost/delayed are different: Stripe documents duplicate/unordered delivery and retries, not a bounded delivery SLA. A future hard financial-age lease must be anchored to the actual provider observation and paid-through; cache hits, failures, retries and positive events must never slide it. At expiry during a Stripe/worker outage, new paid actions fail closed until verified. Unknown/new purchases stay denied. [Webhook delivery guidance](https://docs.stripe.com/webhooks).

This bounds **new authorizations**, not already dispatched work. An already sent provider request cannot be recalled. Queued work must reauthorize immediately before dispatch; long-running work and result publication need current revision checks. Do not claim an end-to-end revocation time until the real job/result paths have an enforced and tested duration/checkpoint bound; otherwise running-work exposure remains unbounded by this table. BYOK removes KXRA-funded inference, not data or infrastructure exposure. The seven-day renewal grace/read-only recovery policy remains a separate unresolved current-terms integration; this candidate does not implement or revoke it.

## Reproduce and remaining gates

Local-only combined command:

```sh
node scripts/billing-lifecycle-acceptance.mjs --synthetic --shadow
```

It creates private fixtures serially, installs the pinned admission artifact and unnumbered shadow proposal, runs service/native cases and verifies/stops both owned servers. `scripts/reduced-shadow-fixture.ts` accepts no arbitrary SQL path or hosted URL and checks explicit fixture mode, private socket, original administrator and no TCP listener. Failure receipts are retained. `npm run worker:reduced-billing-shadow` stays disabled unless the separate environment flag, original runner/profile guards, enabled SQL config and reviewed shared read budget all agree. It creates no deployment, schedule or provider webhook.

Before any production-authority replacement: approve the exact action-specific financial-age policy; integrate a separately reviewed SQL policy/lease and job authorization implementation; preserve refunds, cancellation and eventual grace lineage; allocate/review the migration; run exact-source CI/security review and protected hosted sandbox lifecycle/card/event/clock tests; establish authenticated webhook reachability, verified unmatched-event/resource resolution, cron ownership, whole-account read monitoring and dead-letter response. Shadow observations alone are not evidence that those gates are complete.
