## What actually requires freshness

Required invariants are canonical scope/account/mode/resource/tenant association, verified collected payment and consent, replay-safe receipts, current policy/member authority, cancellation semantics, refund/dispute handling and generation-checked writes. Unknown or failed verification must not create or prolong paid access. Local admin suspension, member revocation and legal/policy withdrawal can be enforced transactionally without waiting for Stripe.

The current source imposes a financial proof age of at most 300 seconds in `live-paid-access.ts` and migration 0104; fixture policies commonly select 120 seconds. Migration 0110 begins refresh near expiry. The inspected accepted commercial policy establishes paid-period-end cancellation and the separate failed-renewal grace commitment; no inspected owner instruction or accepted customer contract establishes a 120/300-second provider-change revocation SLA. This is a source finding, not proof that no other security decision exists. Therefore retain the bound now, but ask for one explicit threat-model decision rather than presenting this numerical constant as a Stripe requirement. Short-lived local authorization and maximum age of external financial evidence are different controls.

## Simpler event-led operating design

Reuse the existing verified inbox/worker/database topology before introducing an always-on high-frequency daemon or new queue product. Stripe recommends asynchronous webhook handling, warns that deliveries can be duplicated or unordered, and retries live deliveries for up to three days. Those retries are not a timely-delivery guarantee. Its subscription guidance associates `invoice.paid` plus active subscription with provisioning; active status alone is insufficient for this application's stricter collected-payment policy. [Webhook guidance](https://docs.stripe.com/webhooks), [subscription events](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/subscriptions/webhooks).

1. Verify signature over bounded raw bytes and validate fixed account/mode. Resolve customer **and subscription/resource** to the canonical server-owned workspace binding. Existing schema makes customer unique per workspace; do not remove that constraint casually. Future shared-customer billing needs a unique subscription/intent mapping, never fanout from customer ID alone. Commit an immutable event ID/digest and durable pending work transactionally before 2xx. Persistence failure receives no success acknowledgement; conflict bytes quarantine. Acknowledge a committed duplicate safely. Never log raw payloads or signatures.
2. Serialize projection changes per binding/resource using SQL locks and a revision. New relevant events coalesce one pending current-state reconciliation. Restrictive events invalidate the local financial lease transactionally; positive or old events only wake verification and cannot grant. Event creation seconds are not a total order. Keep durable retry/backoff, attempts, next due and dead-letter state; retain original event/first-failure lineage across crash/replay.
3. Keep original collected invoice/charge/period and verified assent receipts, plus current revision, paid-through, last provider verification and a hard financial-verification deadline. Grant or extend only from a successful canonical, current-generation provider read. A 429, timeout, failed read, queue retry, cache hit or incoming positive event never moves that deadline. Cancel-at-period-end retains paid access until that end; immediate cancellation/refund/dispute and manual suspension have distinct restrictive paths. Dunning grace remains separately versioned and anchored to first verified renewal failure.
4. Schedule only changed, due, suspect and repair work; use per-binding single-flight, global read/rate/concurrency budgets, jitter and pagination bounds. Reserve capacity for adverse-state repair and checkout. On overload, preserve hard deadlines and expose unavailable status; do not extend access to hide an outage. Every periodic sweep needs a durable cursor and must not starve later tenants. No new external service or credential is intrinsically required.
5. Authorize each generation/export at enqueue and again immediately before provider dispatch/result publication using the current SQL revision/member/legal state. A process-local or cross-instance cache is only a hint; it cannot authorize against an unchecked old revision. Work already sent to a customer's provider cannot be recalled; bound streaming/job duration and reauthorize checkpoints/result delivery. These worker paths need integration tests before claiming a revocation bound on running work. BYOK changes who pays inference, not data disclosure or infrastructure exposure.

## Quantified policy trade-off, not an approval request for excessive polling

For 16 continuously active workspaces over 30 days, periodic reads alone are:

| Maximum financial age / successful reconciliation interval | Existing 20 GET path | Target 4 GET path | Conservative 8 GET path |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 120 seconds | 6,912,000 | 1,382,400 | 2,764,800 |
| 300 seconds | 2,764,800 | 552,960 | 1,105,920 |
| 1 hour | 230,400 | 46,080 | 92,160 |
| 6 hours | 38,400 | 7,680 | 15,360 |
| 24 hours | 9,600 | 1,920 | 3,840 |

These steady-state counts use `workspaces * 2,592,000 / interval * reads`; they exclude initial verification, change events, retries, pagination, configuration audits and other applications. Earlier 172,780/6,911,680 figures allowed a fresh proof at the window start; these round steady-state figures avoid suggesting spare operational headroom. Stripe's allocation is 500 GETs per transaction over rolling 30 days, minimum 10,000 monthly, not a published per-GET price. Transaction count and all other account traffic matter. [Stripe limits](https://docs.stripe.com/rate-limits).

As an explicit planning example, 16 workspaces with a validated eight-GET dynamic graph and a 24-hour hard financial-age bound use 3,840 scheduled reads; one additional coalesced full verification per workspace per day adds 3,840, and 16 initial strict checks add 320: 8,000 total, leaving 2,000 of a 10,000 floor for other calls/retries/audits. This is a bounded workload assumption, not a guarantee; real events are unbounded, so exhaustion must deny new lease extensions. At six-hour/eight-GET cadence the periodic baseline alone already exceeds 10,000. Measure actual traffic and choose admission limits rather than manufacture an allocation increase.

If a provider-side adverse change occurs immediately after a good observation and **every** event is missed, hard financial age `A` limits new authorizations to at most `A` after that observation, capped earlier by paid-through. Thus 6 hours means up to 21,600 seconds; 24 hours means up to 86,400 seconds. Delivered restrictive events invalidate in their durable transaction; delivery/processing time has no guaranteed bound. Add the separately bounded remaining duration of already authorized work and trusted database-clock error to any end-to-end exposure claim. Read/queue failure never adds more time: at the deadline, access fails closed. “Webhooks only until paid-through” could expose almost the entire monthly period and is not recommended.

The decision is therefore **maximum acceptable age of verified financial state for each action**, not “approve millions of GETs.” If the genuine requirement remains 120/300 seconds, preserve expiry and accept availability loss when the account cannot support safe current verification; optimization alone does not make sustained 16-workspace polling fit ordinary allocation. If the owner/security reviewer accepts a longer bounded financial age, version it explicitly with an action-specific risk rationale and retain immediate local suspension and event-triggered invalidation. The 24-hour fixture is an example for review, not an adopted recommendation for every action. Refund/dispute exposure, large exports, new seats, data sharing and admin actions may require fresher evidence than ordinary previously paid work.
